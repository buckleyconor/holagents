# Business value (concept level)

Does this lab make a case a customer would care about, or is it a product
tour with a preamble? Judges the argument, not the prose.

## Criteria

### problem-is-real

The problem is one a named persona actually has, stated with enough
specificity to be recognisable — cost, risk, time, or compliance pressure
they already feel. Not a restatement of what the product does.

### value-is-concrete

What changes for the persona is specific and consequential. "Improves
efficiency" scores low; "proves which restore point is clean before
committing to it" scores high.

### claims-are-defensible

Every claim survives "compared to what?". Comparative and competitive
claims are either supported by something in the research context or
softened to what can be shown. Nothing rests on scraped vendor marketing
treated as neutral evidence.

### claims-are-demonstrable

Every claim survives "how would the learner see that?". A claim the lab
cannot show on screen is either dropped or explicitly marked as context
rather than a demonstrated outcome.

### audience-fit

The stated audience matches the problem and the technical level implied by
the beats. A lab pitched at administrators does not have a beat that
requires writing a model training loop.
